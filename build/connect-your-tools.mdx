title: "Connect your tools"
description: "Connections are the agent's arms — add an app once per account, activate it per workflow, and disconnect it when you no longer want the access."

Connections are the agent's **arms** — they define what it can reach and act on in your systems. Connecting happens on two levels, and both live in the build conversation.

## Before you start

- An account with the app you want to connect, and permission to sign in to it.
- You sign in with your own account, so an agent using that connection reaches exactly what you can reach — no more. [Team connections](/share/team-spaces#connections-in-a-team) are the exception: they belong to the team, not to you.
- The app has to be available in your organization. An admin can block an app or mark it **Restricted**, and a restricted app is usable on request. See [Apps & permissions](/admin/connected-apps-management).

## Level 1: Connect the tool to your account — once

The first time a workflow needs a tool you've never connected, Dataleap tells you and prompts the sign-in right in the chat:

> *"No existing Jira connection in your account, so I'll create a new one — this will prompt an OAuth authorization on your end."*

You click **Connect**, sign in once, done. From then on, that connection exists for your whole account.

<Info>
  You can also set up connections ahead of time under **Settings** > **Connected Apps** — the same place where you reconnect an expired login or review what's connected.
</Info>

### Add an app yourself

<Steps>
<Step title="Open Connected Apps">

Go to **Settings** > **Connected Apps**. Personal and team connections are listed separately.

</Step>
<Step title="Pick the app">

Click **Add Apps**, search the list, and select the app you want. Selecting it shows what the app can do before you commit to anything.

</Step>
<Step title="Sign in and approve the access">

Click **Connect**. The app's own sign-in opens in a new window and asks you to confirm the access Dataleap requested. Approving it there finishes the connection; declining leaves nothing behind.

</Step>
<Step title="Check what you connected">

The app now appears in **Connected Apps** with the account it uses. Expand the row to see which agents use it.

</Step>
</Steps>

## Level 2: Activate it for the workflow — with exactly the permissions needed

An account-level connection doesn't give every agent free rein. For each workflow, the agent requests the **specific tools** it needs from that connection — and you see every single permission before approving:

<Frame caption="Every permission is visible — and the set is as small as the workflow allows.">
  <img src="/images/connections-workflow-permissions.png" alt="The agent requests to activate connections: Gmail and Notion, each listing the exact tools requested, with an Approve All button" />
</Frame>

The gray labels are the permissions — *Create Draft*, *Create Label*, *Download Attachment*, and so on. Dataleap keeps this set **as small as possible**: enough for the workflow to run, and nothing beyond it. One click on **Approve All**, and the agent is equipped.

## Which Slack connection to use

Slack is the one app you can connect twice: once with your personal Slack account, and once org-wide on behalf of your whole organization. Both can show up under **Connected Apps**, and they have different capabilities.

| | Personal connection | Org-wide connection |
|---|---|---|
| Default for | your personal agents | agents in a team space |
| Runs as | you | the organization's Slack bot |
| Best for | searching messages, reading and writing private channels and DMs, acting as you | most day-to-day Slack work: sending and reading messages, managing channels, reacting |
| Can't do | — | search messages, or read private channels and DMs the bot hasn't been added to |

If your agent can't find or read a private channel or DM, it's probably using the org-wide connection. Ask it in the chat:

> *"Please switch from the org-wide Slack connection to my personal Slack connection."*

<Note>
  Your admin may have installed Slack without user permissions — in that case personal connections aren't available, and the org-wide connection is your only option.
</Note>

## What this means in practice

<Steps>
<Step title="You describe the job">

*"Give me a briefing before every meeting, based on my calendar and my emails."*

</Step>
<Step title="Dataleap names what it needs">

*"For that I'll need access to your calendar and your email — please connect your Google Calendar and your Gmail."* New tools get the one-time account connection; known tools go straight to the permission request.

</Step>
<Step title="You approve">

Review the requested permissions and click **Approve All**. Connections are reusable — the next agent that needs Gmail skips level 1 entirely.

</Step>
</Steps>

## What you can ask for once it's connected

- **Work in the chat.** Describe the job and the agent uses the tools it has. You never call a tool by name.
- **Let the app start the run.** Some apps can trigger an agent when something happens in them — a new message, a finished recording, an updated record. See [Triggers](/build/triggers).
- **The app's own rules still apply.** Your agent inherits them: anything that needs a particular plan, licence, or setting in that app stays unavailable until it is enabled there, and the app's own rate limits count against your agent too.

## Troubleshooting

| What you run into | What to do |
|---|---|
| The app you need isn't in the list | It may not be supported yet, or an admin may have blocked it. Use **Request App** to send the name and what you need it for. |
| A tool says it has been restricted by your admin | Send a request with a short reason. An admin accepts or rejects it, and the decision comes back to you. |
| The sign-in stopped working | Connect the app again — the dialog shows **Reconnect** instead of **Connect**. Nothing else about the agent changes. |
| The agent is using the wrong account | Check the account shown next to the app in **Connected Apps**. Disconnect it, then connect again with the account you meant. |
| An agent stopped running after you disconnected an app | Disconnecting switches off the agents that used it. Connect the app again, then switch those agents back on. |
| The agent can't see a private Slack channel or DM | It is using the org-wide connection. Ask it in the chat to switch to your personal one. |

## Disconnect an app

Disconnecting removes Dataleap's access to that account. It does not change or delete anything inside the app itself.

<Steps>
<Step title="Find the connection">

Go to **Settings** > **Connected Apps** and find the app. The row shows the account it uses and how many agents use it.

</Step>
<Step title="Choose Disconnect">

Open the row's menu and click **Disconnect**.

</Step>
<Step title="Decide what happens to the agents">

The dialog lists every agent that will be affected. **Deactivate affected agents** is selected by default, so those agents are switched off rather than left to fail mid-run. Clear it only if you are reconnecting straight away.

</Step>
<Step title="Confirm">

Click **Disconnect App**.

</Step>
</Steps>

After you disconnect:

- The connection and the sign-in stored behind it are deleted, so no agent can reach that account through Dataleap.
- Agents that used it lose that capability. If you left **Deactivate affected agents** selected, they stay switched off until you turn them back on.
- If the connection belonged to a team, it stops being available to everyone in that team.

<Warning>
  Disconnecting ends the access from Dataleap's side. Many apps also keep their own list of connected or authorized apps — if you want the authorization gone on both ends, open that list in the app and remove Dataleap there too.
</Warning>

Workspace-wide governance (reviewing, reconnecting, removing connections centrally) is an admin topic: see [Connected apps management](/admin/connected-apps-management).
